## The Sensory Gap in Traditional Fragrance E-Commerce

Selling fragrance online presents a unique hurdle: you are trying to describe an invisible, subjective, and highly complex chemical compound to a customer who cannot smell it.

Traditional e-commerce platforms were built for visual or specification-driven products (like sizes for shoes or voltages for lightbulbs). When forced to handle olfactive data, traditional collections and filter trees fail due to several key factors:

- **Olfactive Architecture:** A single perfume is not a static scent; it is a three-tiered pyramid comprising top notes (e.g., bergamot), heart notes (e.g., lavender), and base notes (e.g., vetiver). Standard checkboxes cannot display this temporal progression.
- **Environmental Context:** Scent performance is highly dependent on season, setting, skin chemistry, sillage (scent trail), and longevity. A summer office perfume requires a completely different formulation than a winter date-night scent.
- **The Vocabulary Disconnect:** Everyday shoppers use subjective, feeling-oriented descriptors (e.g., "clean laundry," "cozy fireplace," "dark academia"). Catalog tags are typically highly technical (e.g., "synthetic musk," "amberwood," "coumarin").

When a fragrance retailer has thousands of SKUs, manually tagging every variant for every subjective feeling is impossible. The result is a catalog where 95% of the inventory remains buried and invisible—a phenomenon known as "ghost inventory."
