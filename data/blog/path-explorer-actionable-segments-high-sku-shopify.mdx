Look.

You run a catalog with thousands of SKUs. People land, browse a few products, sometimes search, sometimes open the agent, and then disappear. Shopify shows you sessions. Your email tool shows you purchasers. The middle is fog.

That fog is expensive on high-SKU stores. The long tail never gets seen. Cart abandoners stay anonymous. Searchers who typed the wrong words never get a second chance. Chat-assisted buyers who already proved they convert sit in the same pile as everyone else.

Path Explorer exists to clear the fog.

## What Path Explorer actually does

It tracks full customer journeys across the storefront and the on-page agent. Then it surfaces four segments that matter:

- Product browsers who never added to cart
- Cart abandoners
- Searchers who left without opening a product
- Chat-assisted buyers

These are not vanity metrics. They are built from real sessions that ShopGuide can link to a Shopify Customer ID or email when the shopper identifies themselves (order, login, or chat).

Once a segment is reachable, you can sync it.

One click applies a clear tag in Shopify Admin (something like `path-explorer:2026-08-21:chat-assisted-buyers`). Or it pushes the emails and properties into Klaviyo so you can build a flow or an audience.

No CSV export. No manual matching. The same system that already attributes revenue to the agent now hands you the people behind the numbers.

## Why this matters on large catalogs

Talk to merchants with 5,000 or 50,000 SKUs and the pattern is consistent. Discovery is hard. Filters break. Search returns noise or zero. A lot of high-intent traffic never reaches a product page that fits.

You already pay for that traffic. Path Explorer shows you where it stalled and who is still reachable.

Chat-assisted buyers are the cleanest example. They used the agent, found something, and ordered. Those people already demonstrated that guided discovery works for them. Tagging them lets you nurture the next purchase or the complementary items the agent would have suggested if the cart had more context.

Cart abandoners and product browsers are the recovery opportunity. Most of them stay anonymous. The ones who left an email or placed a previous order become a list you can actually email or retarget without guessing.

Searchers who left tell you what language your catalog is missing. The top terms that produced no product open are the same terms the agent should learn to clarify.

None of this requires a new data warehouse. It uses the sessions and attribution ShopGuide already collects.

## The honest limits

Path Explorer only tags people it can identify. Purely anonymous browsers stay in the aggregate heat maps and funnels. That is by design. You cannot email someone who never gave you an address.

Bot traffic is filtered so the numbers line up with Shopify's human session count. Segments always exclude bots.

And the value depends on what you do after the sync. A tag that sits unused in Shopify Admin is just a tag. The lift comes when you build the Klaviyo flow or the Shopify customer segment and actually talk to those people.

Cleaner product data still helps the agent upstream. Path Explorer does not invent better recommendations. It shows you who already responded to the ones you have.

## Where this sits in agentic commerce

Harley Finkelstein has been clear that agentic commerce is merit-based. Agents match products to intent instead of who paid the most for the keyword. The same logic applies after the session ends.

John Collison called keyword search a ridiculous way to find things to buy. He is right. The on-page agent already improves the discovery half. Path Explorer closes the loop on the people who showed intent and then left, or who converted with help and are ready for the next step.

You keep the traffic you already pay for. You stop treating every visitor as a black box. The agent guides. Path Explorer tells you who listened and who is still reachable.

That is the feature. Not another dashboard full of charts. Four segments and a way to act on them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How does Path Explorer identify customers?**  
It links anonymous sessions to Shopify Customer IDs and emails through order attribution and chat interactions. Only sessions that produce a known identity become part of the reachable segments.

**What are the four main segments?**  
Product browsers who never added to cart, cart abandoners, searchers who left without opening a product, and chat-assisted buyers. Each one has a clear next action in Shopify or Klaviyo.

**Does the Shopify tag let me build customer segments?**  
Yes. After the sync you can filter customers in Shopify Admin with `customer_tags CONTAINS 'path-explorer'`. That is enough to create a segment for LTV checks, repeat purchase campaigns, or exclusion lists.

**Can I send the same people to Klaviyo?**  
Yes. Path Explorer can push emails and properties (cohort, label, etc.) into a designated Klaviyo list so you can trigger flows or build ad audiences without leaving the app.

**Are bots included in the numbers?**  
No. Analytics can toggle bot exclusion to match Shopify's human session count. Segments are always bot-free.

**Is this useful below 1,000 SKUs?**  
The pain is sharpest on high-SKU catalogs where discovery friction creates more abandoned paths. Smaller stores still get the same segments and sync tools if they want them.
