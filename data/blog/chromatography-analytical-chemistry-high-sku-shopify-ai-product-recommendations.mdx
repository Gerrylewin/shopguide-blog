## The Flaw in Legacy Search: Why Filters Fail High-SKU Science Catalogs

In analytical chemistry, subtle differences determine whether an experiment succeeds or fails. Selecting an unmodified silica C18 column when an end-capped or polar-embedded stationary phase is required causes peak tailing, irreversible compound retention, and column bleeding in mass spectrometers.

Standard e-commerce discovery was built around consumer items where shoppers scan pictures of apparel or home goods. In contrast, scientific procurement is driven by strict technical matrices:

- **Stationary Phase Chemistry:** Octadecyl (C18), C8, Phenyl-Hexyl, HILIC, or Chiral selectors.
- **Pore Dimension & Surface Area:** 80 Å to 300 Å pore sizes matching analyte molecular weights (small molecules vs. large intact monoclonal antibodies).
- **Mechanical & Fluidic Limits:** Operating pressure tolerances up to 15,000 psi (UHPLC) versus 6,000 psi (standard analytical HPLC).
- **Fitting Geometries & Dead Volume:** 10-32 thread PEEK finger-tight nuts versus high-pressure stainless steel ferrules with zero-dead-volume (ZDV) unions.

When a laboratory supply catalog scales past 10,000 SKUs, managing these multidimensional parameters through static collection tags becomes impossible. Dropdown menus multiply into an unusable filter wall, and customers experience decision fatigue.
