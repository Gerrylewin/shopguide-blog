Look.

You run a Shopify store with thousands of SKUs. Traffic arrives. People land on a collection, try a couple filters, type something into search, maybe open a product page, and leave.

Your analytics say bounce rate is high. Cool. Which page actually killed the session? Which path turned into a dead end most often? You cannot tell from a simple bounce number.

Path Explorer includes a Journey Heat Map that answers those questions with the real paths your shoppers took.

## One feature: the Journey Heat Map

The Heat Map sits inside Path Explorer. It visualizes session flows between pages. You can zoom, pan, and hover to see drop-off rates and traffic percentages at each step.

It is not a generic funnel. It is built from the actual sequences of page views, filter use, search attempts, and chat interactions that happened on your store. When a shopper engages the on-page agent, that shows up in the path too.

You see the common entry points. You see the pages that receive the most traffic and the ones that leak the most. You see where guided sessions differ from pure browse sessions.

Bot traffic can be excluded so the picture stays clean.

## Why this matters on a high-SKU catalog

Merchants with large catalogs talk about the same friction on Reddit and in Shopify forums. Search returns zero results for real intent. Filters max out or disappear. Long-tail products stay invisible. Shoppers bounce because they cannot find the right variant, the right fit, or the right complementary item.

A simple bounce rate does not tell you whether the problem is the collection page, the search results, the product detail page, or the moment after they open chat. The Heat Map does.

Once you can see the dead ends you can act:

- Fix the collection or product page that loses the most sessions
- Feed the common stuck points back into agent training or launch messages
- Spot which paths convert when the agent steps in
- Stop guessing which part of the store needs the most attention

That is the difference between looking at a chart and running the store.

## Honest limits

The Heat Map is only as complete as the sessions that reach it. Anonymous browsing stays in aggregate until identity is linked by an order or a clear capture. Very short sessions or pure bot noise can still appear if you do not toggle the exclude bots setting. Clean product data and consistent metafields make the agent guidance stronger, which in turn changes the paths the Heat Map records.

None of that is unique to ShopGuide. It is the normal reality of storefront data. The Heat Map just makes the useful signal visible without a separate analytics stack.

## Where this sits in agentic commerce

Harley Finkelstein has been clear about the shift:

> "Agentic is fundamentally merit-based as opposed to, if you go to a search engine, you type sneakers, you are going to see Footlocker."

Discovery starts rewarding the best fit instead of the biggest ad budget. The stores that win are the ones that can see which journeys actually produced that fit and which ones stalled.

The Journey Heat Map is the diagnostic layer for the on-page agent that already knows your live catalog. You stop arguing about engagement and start fixing the specific pages and sequences that leak revenue on a high-SKU catalog.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How is the Journey Heat Map different from Shopify Analytics?**  
Shopify Analytics gives solid overall numbers. The Heat Map focuses on the sequence of pages and interactions inside the guided and non-guided sessions so you can see the exact drop-off points.

**Does it include chat interactions?**  
Yes. When a shopper engages the on-page agent the interaction appears in the path. You can compare guided sequences against pure browse sequences.

**Can I exclude bot traffic?**  
Yes. There is a toggle that filters using Shopify verified human session counts so the map stays useful.

**What if most of my traffic is anonymous?**  
You still get aggregate path and drop-off insight. Named segments and richer identity grow as more shoppers convert or identify themselves.

**Is this only useful for stores with 1,000+ SKUs?**  
The value is highest where discovery is hard and journeys are messy, which is the normal case for high-SKU catalogs. Smaller stores still get a clean view of the sessions that do happen.

**Do I need developers to use this?**  
No. The agent is an app block. Path Explorer and the Heat Map live in the ShopGuide dashboard once the app is installed and the block is live.
